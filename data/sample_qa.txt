Q: What is RAG?
A: RAG stands for Retrieval-Augmented Generation, a technique that combines retrieval of relevant documents with language model generation.

Q: What are the benefits of RAG?
A: RAG improves accuracy by grounding responses in retrieved documents, reducing hallucinations and improving factual correctness.

Q: What is LangChain?
A: LangChain is an open-source framework for building applications powered by large language models.

Q: What is FAISS?
A: FAISS is a library developed by Facebook AI Research for efficient similarity search and clustering of dense vectors.

Q: What is an embedding?
A: An embedding is a numerical vector representation of text that captures semantic meaning, allowing similar texts to be close together in vector space.

Q: What is a vector database?
A: A vector database stores and indexes high-dimensional vectors (embeddings) and enables fast similarity search over them.

Q: What is GPT-4o-mini?
A: GPT-4o-mini is a lightweight and cost-efficient version of OpenAI's GPT-4o model, designed for tasks requiring fast and affordable text generation.

Q: How does RAG reduce hallucinations?
A: RAG reduces hallucinations by retrieving factual documents and providing them as context to the language model, grounding its responses in real information.

Q: What is the role of a retriever in RAG?
A: The retriever in RAG is responsible for finding the most relevant documents from a knowledge base given a user query, typically using vector similarity search.

Q: What is the role of a generator in RAG?
A: The generator in RAG is the language model that takes the retrieved documents as context and generates a coherent, informative answer to the user's question.
